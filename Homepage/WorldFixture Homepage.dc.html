A fake company your software can interact with.

Run local, emulated Slack, GitHub, Gmail, Stripe, S3, and more with shared people and history. New activity arrives over time, so you can see how your app responds.

You build an app that connects to Slack, Gmail, or Stripe. To test it, you need accounts, data in those accounts, and activity for your app to respond to.

WorldFixture gives you local, fake versions of those services, already populated with connected data. New events arrive while your app runs.

Point your app at them, check that it works, then reset and test again.

Before you use it

Who is WorldFixture for?

Developers building apps that read from or write to external services.

What can I use it for?

Test workflows across services, develop an integration, or demo your app with realistic activity.

When is it useful?

Before a customer demo, while building an integration, or when tests need the same starting data. It helps when people and records must match across several services.

When are normal tests a better fit?

Use unit tests and mocks for isolated logic, and a real test database for database behavior. Keep provider sandbox checks: WorldFixture implements selected API operations, not complete copies of each provider.

How does it compare to other tools?

Faker generates data. WireMock lets you define API behavior. WorldFixture provides a company across local services, with shared history and events. It builds on emulate.dev for many of its APIs.

What would a product demo look like?

Connect your app to local services. Show it reading the existing data and responding as new events arrive.
